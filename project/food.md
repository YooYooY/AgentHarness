# Chapter 2 — Food Core Shadowing Passages

## IELTS 6.5 Speaking and Vocabulary Collection

This collection contains 12 passages based on the six vocabulary units in Chapter 2. Each topic has two passages:

- **Passage A — Everyday English:** personal experience, habits, or a real-life situation.
- **Passage B — IELTS English:** explanation, comparison, causes, effects, or solutions.

Each passage is written at approximately IELTS 6.5 level. The language is natural and reusable rather than deliberately advanced.

## How to practise each passage

1. Read it once for meaning.
2. Learn the core chunks rather than isolated words.
3. Divide the passage into the marked shadowing sections.
4. Shadow each section three times.
5. Shadow the whole passage without stopping.
6. Close the text and retell the main ideas.
7. Replace the original information with your own experience or opinion.

# Theme 1 — Daily Meals, Fruit and Vegetables

## Passage 1 — What I Normally Eat in a Day

### Core passage

I also keep bananas or apples at home because they make a quick and nutritious snack. My diet is not perfect, and I still eat fast food occasionally. However, I try to maintain a reasonable balance instead of following a strict diet that would be difficult to continue.

## Passage 2 — Why a Balanced Diet Matters

### Core passage

A balanced diet does not mean eating perfectly at every meal. Instead, it means getting a suitable variety of nutrients over time. Fresh fruit and vegetables provide vitamins and fibre, while eggs, fish, meat, and beans are useful sources of protein. Rice, oats, bread, and other grains give us the energy we need for work and study. Problems may arise when people depend too heavily on processed food, which often contains excessive amounts of sugar, salt, and unhealthy fat. In the short term, poor eating habits can make people feel tired and affect their concentration. Over time, they may increase the risk of obesity and other health problems. In my opinion, the most practical approach is not to ban particular foods. People should improve their everyday meals, control portion sizes, and treat sugary or greasy food as an occasional choice rather than a daily habit.

## Passage 4 — Should People Eat Less Meat?

### Core passage

Many people are trying to reduce the amount of meat they eat. One reason is health. Processed meat, such as bacon and sausages, can contain high levels of fat and salt, while eating too much red meat may be harmful in the long term. Environmental concerns also influence people’s choices because producing meat generally requires more land and water than growing vegetables or grains. However, this does not necessarily mean that everyone must become vegetarian. Meat, fish, and poultry are convenient sources of protein and other nutrients. A more realistic solution may be to eat smaller portions, choose lean meat, and replace some meat-based meals with beans, eggs, or vegetables. Personally, I still enjoy chicken, beef, and seafood, but I try to eat a wider variety of foods. For most people, moderation is probably easier to maintain than completely giving up meat.

## Passage 6 — Why Taste Preferences Are Different

### Core passage

People’s taste preferences are shaped by several factors. The food we eat during childhood plays an important role because familiar flavors often make us feel comfortable. Local cuisine matters as well. Someone who grows up eating chili may have a much higher tolerance for spicy food than someone who rarely eats it. Personal experience can also change our preferences. A person may dislike bitter vegetables as a child but begin to enjoy them as an adult. Health concerns influence taste in another way. After reducing sugar or salt for a period of time, people sometimes find that heavily seasoned food tastes too strong. Although taste is personal, it is not completely fixed. Trying unfamiliar food several times may gradually make it more acceptable. This is why travelling, living abroad, or eating with people from different cultures can greatly expand the range of food we enjoy.

# Theme 6 — Food Health, Storage and Safety

## Passage 12 — Improving Food Safety and Public Health

### Core passage

Food safety is essential because contaminated food can affect a large number of people. Problems may occur during production, transport, storage, or preparation. For example, raw meat can contain harmful bacteria, and food may spoil if it is not kept at the correct temperature. Clear rules are therefore needed throughout the food supply chain. Producers should maintain hygienic conditions, shops must check storage temperatures, and restaurants need to keep raw and cooked food separate. Consumers also have responsibilities. They should wash their hands, check expiry dates, refrigerate food promptly, and cook meat thoroughly. Governments can support these efforts through regular inspections and clear food labels. Although no system can remove every risk, proper regulation and sensible daily habits can greatly reduce cases of food poisoning and help people make safer, more informed choices.

- Did I use complete sentences rather than isolated words?

- Did I give a reason or example?
- Did I use the correct tense?
